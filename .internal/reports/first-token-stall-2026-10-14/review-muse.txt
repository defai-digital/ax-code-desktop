2. **Could the client add 40–160 s pre-first-token?**

   Mostly no for the mechanisms as sized: hashing/serializing 335 KB is milliseconds; 282 requests/286 s is trivial HTTP load. The credible client mechanism is **event-loop blocking in the Node runtime** — synchronous SQLite operations on a 10 GB DB, WAL checkpoints, or repeated expensive session-list/history queries from the polling GUI — which can stall processing of an already-arrived stream. 40–160 s from this alone would require severe repeated blocking, so it's a contributor suspect, not the leading one.

   With only your four signals: `setupMs` = 6.8 ms rules out pre-dispatch client work (request building, hashing) almost completely — the stall lives after dispatch. A provider stall shows high `firstContentMs` with normal streaming thereafter (`streamMs − firstContentMs` proportional to output); client read-blocking shows the same aggregate, plus bursty catch-up delivery — but aggregates can't separate them. The provider error stream is your one asymmetric signal: client blocking never generates `AI_APICallError: upstream request failed`, so genuine upstream failures are confirmed; they corroborate, but don't prove, that the silent stalls are also upstream.

3. **Yes, `firstContentMs` can hide a client stall.**

   If it's timestamped when the adapter's chunk callback *runs* on the Node event loop, bytes sitting in the kernel socket buffer during a loop block (SQLite, GC, a polling handler) are recorded as provider latency. It also hides retries, connection setup, and gateway queueing indiscriminately — it's a wall-clock between two client-side points, not a wire measurement.

   Settling instrumentation: monotonic timestamps at four layers — request dispatched, first byte on the socket (undici/TCP-level instrumentation or a packet capture), first chunk parsed by the adapter, first token rendered — plus `perf_hooks.monitorEventLoopDelay()` and per-attempt SDK logging. If socket-first-byte ≈ adapter-first-chunk on slow turns, the client is exonerated; a gap between them is the client stall, sized exactly.
